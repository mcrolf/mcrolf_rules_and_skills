# Writing

## Product copy

Before writing customer-facing copy, use the product brief, existing UI, and code to identify what the feature actually does and who it is for. Do not infer benefits or capabilities from a section heading or visual design. If the facts are insufficient, write a neutral placeholder or flag the missing information.

Write product copy as interface text, not as a pitch. Avoid template-driven copy that sounds polished but uses familiar marketing shapes instead of saying precisely what the product does.

- Use only facts established in the project or brief. Name the user's action and the concrete result.
- Prefer ordinary sentences over slogans, metaphors, feature piles, or claims of expertise. Use the project's domain language and words customers use for the task.
- Do not add a punchy closing sentence just to complete a section.
- If a product detail is unknown, use a clearly marked placeholder or ask. Do not invent a benefit.
- Do not require copy for every visual slot. A sparse page with accurate text is usually better than a full page of invented marketing prose. Remove or leave a slot empty when there is nothing useful and established to say.

## Product copy review

Before sending or saving product copy, review each sentence and revise or remove anything that fails these tests:

- Could this sentence describe ten unrelated SaaS products? If so, make it more specific.
- Does it claim a result the product may not reliably deliver?
- Does it use a colon to introduce a polished list of benefits? Try a plain sentence instead.
- Would a customer use these words to describe the task?
- Is the sentence helping someone decide or act, or merely filling the design?

## Punctuation in all agent writing

- Do not use em dashes (U+2014) in chat, progress updates, documentation, comments, commit messages, or other generated writing.
- Do not use an en dash (U+2013) to separate clauses, set off an aside, or break a sentence. En dashes are only for numeric or date ranges.
- Use a comma, parentheses, a colon, a semicolon, or a new sentence. Hyphens in compounds (`well-known`, `user-defined`) are fine.
- Before sending or saving, replace any em dash or sentence-break en dash.

Example: "The update is ready, pending review."
